EU AI Act: High-Risk AI Systems

The European Union Artificial Intelligence Act (EU AI Act) classifies AI
systems into risk categories. High-risk AI systems are subject to the most
stringent regulatory obligations. Understanding the classification criteria and
compliance requirements is essential for reviewing contracts involving AI.

An AI system is classified as high-risk under two main categories. First,
systems that are safety components of products or fall under EU harmonization
legislation requiring third-party conformity assessment. Second, stand-alone AI
systems in specific high-risk use cases: biometric identification and
categorization of natural persons; management and operation of critical
infrastructure; education and vocational training; employment, worker
management, and access to self-employment; access to essential private and
public services including credit scoring; law enforcement; migration, asylum,
and border control management; and administration of justice and democratic
processes.

Providers of high-risk AI systems must implement a risk management system
throughout the system lifecycle. This includes identifying and analyzing known
and foreseeable risks, evaluating the acceptability of residual risks, and
implementing risk management measures. The risk management process must be
documented and continuously updated.

Data governance obligations require that training, validation, and testing
datasets are relevant, representative, and free from errors to the extent
possible. Appropriate data governance practices must address dataset design
choices, bias detection and mitigation, and data adequacy assessments.

Technical documentation must be prepared before market placement and kept up to
date. It must include a general description of the system, detailed design
information, development methodology, and results of conformity assessment
procedures. Transparency and provision of information obligations require that
deployers understand the capabilities and limitations of the system.

Human oversight measures must enable deployers to effectively monitor the
system, understand its outputs, and override or stop the system when
necessary. Accuracy, robustness, and cybersecurity requirements demand that
high-risk systems achieve appropriate performance levels and are resilient
against errors and attacks.

Record-keeping and logging capabilities must be built into the system to enable
traceability and post-market monitoring. Providers must establish a post-market
monitoring system and report serious incidents to national competent
authorities.

In contract review, identify whether the contract involves the provision or
deployment of an AI system that could qualify as high-risk. Contracts should
allocate responsibility for risk management, data governance, human oversight,
and documentation obligations between provider and deployer. Warranty clauses
should address compliance with the EU AI Act technical and organizational
requirements.
