Confidentiality Clause Reference

Confidentiality clauses, also known as non-disclosure provisions, protect
sensitive information exchanged between parties during the course of a
contractual relationship. These clauses are essential for safeguarding trade
secrets, business strategies, customer data, and other proprietary information.

Definition of confidential information: The clause must clearly define what
constitutes confidential information. Common approaches include blanket
definitions covering all information disclosed, specific enumerated categories,
or a combination requiring written marking or oral designation. Best practice
is to define information by its nature and the circumstances of disclosure
rather than relying solely on marking requirements.

Exclusions: Standard exclusions cover information that is or becomes publicly
known through no fault of the receiving party, already known by the receiving
party prior to disclosure, independently developed without use of confidential
information, or required to be disclosed by law or court order. Permitted
disclosures should also include disclosure to employees, agents, and advisors
who need to know on a need-to-know basis.

Obligations of the receiving party: The receiving party must maintain
confidential information in strict confidence, use it only for the permitted
purpose, restrict access to authorized personnel, and return or destroy
confidential information upon termination. Reasonable care obligations should
not be less than the standard used for the receiving party's own confidential
information.

Duration: Confidentiality obligations may survive termination of the main
agreement. Trade secret protection typically lasts as long as the information
remains a trade secret. Non-trade secret information may have a fixed
post-termination period such as two to five years. The duration should be
commercially reasonable and enforceable under applicable law.

Security measures: The clause should require reasonable security measures to
protect confidential information from unauthorized access, disclosure, or
breach. This may include encryption, access controls, secure storage, and
incident notification procedures. Data protection obligations under GDPR may
impose additional requirements.

Remedies: Breach of confidentiality can cause irreparable harm. The clause
should acknowledge that monetary damages may be inadequate and that injunctive
relief is appropriate. Some clauses include liquidated damages for specific
breaches.

Compelled disclosure: If the receiving party is legally required to disclose
confidential information, it must notify the disclosing party promptly,
cooperate in seeking a protective order, and disclose only the minimum
information required.

In contract review, verify that confidentiality clauses are mutual or
one-sided as appropriate, that the definition of confidential information
covers the specific categories of data being shared, that exclusions are not
overly broad, and that post-termination obligations are clearly stated.
