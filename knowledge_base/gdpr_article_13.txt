GDPR Article 13: Information to Be Provided When Personal Data Are Collected
from the Data Subject

Article 13 of the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) specifies the
information that controllers must provide to data subjects when personal data
is collected directly from them. This transparency obligation applies at the
time the data is obtained. The goal is to ensure that individuals have full
visibility into how their personal data will be processed.

Under Article 13(1), the controller must provide the following information at
the time of collection: the identity and contact details of the controller and,
where applicable, the controller's representative; the contact details of the
Data Protection Officer; the purposes of the processing and the lawful basis
for the processing; the legitimate interests pursued by the controller or a
third party where the processing is based on legitimate interests; and the
recipients or categories of recipients of the personal data, if any.

Under Article 13(2), additional information must be provided to ensure fair and
transparent processing. This includes: the period for which the personal data
will be stored, or the criteria used to determine that period; the existence of
the right to request access, rectification, erasure, restriction of processing,
data portability, and the right to object to processing; the right to withdraw
consent at any time where processing is based on consent; the right to lodge a
complaint with a supervisory authority; whether providing personal data is a
statutory or contractual requirement and the possible consequences of failing
to provide the data; and the existence of automated decision-making including
profiling, with meaningful information about the logic involved and the
envisaged consequences.

Where the controller intends to further process personal data for a purpose
other than that for which it was collected, Article 13(3) requires the
controller to provide the data subject with information about that other
purpose and any relevant further information under Article 13(2) before that
processing occurs.

Article 13 information must be concise, transparent, intelligible, and easily
accessible, using clear and plain language. This is especially important when
addressing children. The information should be provided in writing, or by other
appropriate means including electronic means. Oral provision is permitted only
when the identity of the data subject is proven by other means.

In contract review, privacy notices and data collection clauses should be
verified against Article 13 requirements. Contracts involving data processing
should allocate responsibility for providing Article 13 notices to the
controller and should include provisions ensuring that all required information
is delivered to data subjects in a timely and compliant manner.
