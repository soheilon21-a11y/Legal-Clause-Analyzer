GDPR Article 5: Principles Relating to Processing of Personal Data

Article 5 of the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) establishes the
seven core principles that govern all processing of personal data. These
principles form the foundation of European data protection law and apply to
every controller and processor handling personal data of individuals within the
European Union.

The first principle is lawfulness, fairness, and transparency. Personal data
must be processed lawfully, meaning the controller must have a valid lawful
basis such as consent, contractual necessity, legal obligation, vital
interests, public interest, or legitimate interests. Processing must be fair
and not detrimental to the data subject. Transparency requires that data
subjects be informed about how their data is collected, used, and shared in a
clear and accessible manner.

The second principle is purpose limitation. Personal data may only be collected
for specified, explicit, and legitimate purposes. Further processing that is
incompatible with the original purposes is prohibited. Archiving in the public
interest, scientific or historical research, and statistical purposes are not
considered incompatible provided appropriate safeguards exist.

The third principle is data minimization. Controllers must ensure that
processed personal data is adequate, relevant, and limited to what is necessary
for the purposes for which it is processed. This principle discourages
collecting data on the assumption it might become useful later.

The fourth principle is accuracy. Controllers must take every reasonable step
to ensure that inaccurate personal data is erased or rectified without delay.
Data quality processes and regular reviews are essential for compliance.

The fifth principle is storage limitation. Personal data may be kept only for
as long as necessary for the processing purposes. Retention periods must be
defined and justified. After the retention period ends, data must be deleted or
anonymized.

The sixth principle is integrity and confidentiality. Personal data must be
processed in a manner that ensures appropriate security, including protection
against unauthorized or unlawful processing and against accidental loss,
destruction, or damage, using appropriate technical or organizational measures.

The seventh principle is accountability. The controller is responsible for and
must be able to demonstrate compliance with all of the above principles.
Accountability requires documented policies, data protection impact
assessments, records of processing activities, and, where applicable,
appointment of a Data Protection Officer.

When reviewing contracts for GDPR compliance, assess whether obligations
relating to each of these principles are clearly allocated between the
controller and the processor. Data processing agreements should explicitly
reference Article 5 principles and define how each party will uphold them.
