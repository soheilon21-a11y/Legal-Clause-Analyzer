GDPR Data Protection Principles Overview

The General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) is built on seven data protection
principles codified in Article 5. These principles apply to any processing of
personal data and serve as the interpretive framework for the entire
Regulation. Understanding these principles is essential for drafting and
reviewing data protection clauses in contracts.

Lawfulness, fairness, and transparency: Processing must have a valid lawful
basis. The six lawful bases are consent, contract performance, legal
obligation, vital interests, public task, and legitimate interests. Fair
processing means controllers cannot process data in ways that are detrimental
or unexpected. Transparency requires clear communication about how data is
collected, used, and shared.

Purpose limitation: Data must be collected for specified, explicit, and
legitimate purposes. Further processing for incompatible purposes is
prohibited. Contracts should clearly define the purposes for which personal
data is processed and restrict the processor from using data for any other
purpose.

Data minimization: Only personal data that is adequate, relevant, and limited
to what is necessary for the stated purposes may be processed. Contracts should
limit the categories of personal data transferred to the minimum required and
prohibit excessive collection.

Accuracy: Inaccurate personal data must be erased or rectified without delay.
Processors should be required to assist controllers in keeping data accurate
and up to date.

Storage limitation: Personal data may be retained only as long as necessary for
the processing purposes. Retention schedules should be defined in contracts
and data should be deleted or anonymized once the retention period expires.

Integrity and confidentiality: Appropriate security measures must protect
personal data against unauthorized or unlawful processing and against
accidental loss, destruction, or damage. Contracts must specify security
obligations, encryption requirements, access controls, and breach notification
procedures.

Accountability: Controllers are responsible for demonstrating compliance with
all principles. This requires documented policies, data protection impact
assessments, records of processing activities, and potentially a Data
Protection Officer. Contracts should include audit rights, reporting
obligations, and indemnification for non-compliance.

These principles influence every aspect of GDPR compliance. When reviewing
contracts, identify which party acts as controller and which as processor, and
verify that contractual terms allocate responsibilities for each principle.
Standard contractual clauses, binding corporate rules, and data processing
agreements should all align with the Article 5 principles.
