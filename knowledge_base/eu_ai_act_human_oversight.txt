EU AI Act: Human Oversight

Human oversight is a cornerstone of the EU Artificial Intelligence Act. The
Regulation requires that high-risk AI systems be designed and developed so that
natural persons can effectively oversee their operation. This ensures that AI
systems do not undermine human autonomy and that decisions with significant
impact on individuals remain subject to human judgment.

Article 14 of the EU AI Act establishes the human oversight requirement.
Providers must identify and implement appropriate oversight measures before
placing systems on the market. The oversight measures must be built into the
system by design or, where this is not technically feasible, be implemented by
the deployer using appropriate technical and procedural mechanisms.

Human oversight measures must enable two core capabilities. First, the
oversight person must be able to fully understand the system capabilities and
limitations, including its intended purpose, expected behavior, potential
biases, accuracy levels, and circumstances under which the system may produce
erroneous or unreliable outputs. Second, the oversight person must be able to
monitor the system during operation, detect anomalies and unexpected behavior,
and intervene or override the system output when necessary.

Three models of human oversight are recognized. Human-in-the-loop ensures that
the system cannot take action without human intervention. Human-on-the-loop
allows the system to operate autonomously but enables human supervision and
intervention at any time. Human-in-command places overall control with a human
who can decide which tasks to delegate and which to perform directly.

The oversight person must have the necessary competence, training, and
authority to exercise oversight effectively. They must not be subject to
organizational pressures that could compromise their judgment. Deployers are
responsible for assigning oversight responsibilities to qualified personnel and
ensuring that oversight processes are documented and auditable.

When reviewing contracts involving AI systems, verify that human oversight
obligations are clearly defined. The contract should specify whether the
provider or deployer is responsible for implementing oversight measures, what
training and documentation will be provided, what override capabilities exist,
and how oversight incidents are recorded and reported. Warranty and liability
clauses should address failures in human oversight mechanisms. The absence of
human oversight language may indicate non-compliance with the EU AI Act.
